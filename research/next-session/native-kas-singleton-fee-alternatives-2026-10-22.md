# Independent native-KAS exits: verifier cost and established alternatives

Primary-source review, 2026-10-22, 21:17–21:26 UTC. External work was read-only. No build, proof, owner workflow, replay, funding, broadcast or architecture change was performed. The earlier experimental results remain pinned to their original dependency/profile identities.

**Finding:** no reviewed existing mechanism makes a fresh native Groth16 verification fit inside a 60M-sompi singleton at the 500-sompi/gram scenario rate. This follows from the current opcode tariff, independently of prover speed or aggregation software. It is a bound on that verification path, not an impossibility result for every privacy architecture.

There are meaningful alternatives, but each changes an explicit condition: authenticate an exit earlier and claim it later; amortize settlement; require a larger balance or lower fee-demand range; provide explicit fee funding; use narrower privacy; or change native consensus/verifier support. None should be described as an already available cheap shielded-KAS singleton exit under all the original conditions.

## What the lower bound actually says

Current [`tags.rs`](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/crypto/txscript/src/zk_precompiles/tags.rs#L5-L38) accepts only Groth16 `0x20` and RISC0 Succinct `0x21`, charging 140,000 and 250,000 grams respectively. The generic Groth16 verifier additionally charges 250,000 script units for each `gamma_abc_g1` point, requiring exactly `n + 1` points for `n` public scalars. It rejects an empty coefficient list and arity mismatch. This is consensus execution metering, not a prover invoice. [Exact checked-key implementation](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/crypto/txscript/src/zk_precompiles/groth16/mod.rs#L16-L61).

There are 100 script units/gram and 100 grams/compute-budget unit. An input receives 9,999 free script units, so its allowed execution is `10,000 * budget + 9,999`. The allowance cannot pay for another complete budget unit. Native v1 compute mass charges the committed budget plus serialized transaction and output-SPK mass; total relevant mass may be higher in another dimension. [Unit constants/allowance](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/consensus/core/src/mass/units.rs#L4-L23), [allowed units](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/consensus/core/src/tx.rs#L95-L106), [mass equation](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/consensus/core/src/mass/mod.rs#L353-L390).

For a successful fresh Groth16 call the verifier/key component is therefore at least `140,000 + 2,500 * (n + 1)` grams, before surrounding script and transaction costs. The retained direct v6 statement uses two public scalars, hence 147,500 grams. Its true result and ordinary script work do not make this charge disappear.

| Component bound, excluding surrounding transaction costs | Grams | At 100 sompi/g | At 200 | At 500 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Groth16 tag base alone | 140,000 | 14M | 28M | **70M** |
| Smallest nonempty key, zero explicit public scalars, theoretical only | 142,500 | 14.25M | 28.5M | **71.25M** |
| Direct v6, two public scalars | 147,500 | 14.75M | 29.5M | **73.75M** |
| Existing RISC0 Groth16 wrapper, five public scalars | 155,000 | 15.5M | 31M | **77.5M** |
| RISC0 Succinct tag base alone | 250,000 | 25M | 50M | **125M** |

The zero-scalar row is only a lower-bound illustration. It is not a usable replacement for binding a dynamic state/amount/transaction claim. Reducing public inputs cannot overcome even the 140,000-gram base. Replacing RISC0 with another prover or compressing its seal into Groth16 still invokes this tariff. Implementing a faster host/GPU verifier does not change the fixed charge.

**The 500 rate is a scenario, not the current protocol's fixed fee or a measured market quote.** The inspected node's default relay rate is 100 sompi/gram. Relay policy prices the normalized maximum of compute and transient mass; mining/inclusion prioritization can also depend on contextual mass and congestion. Consensus validity, relay admission and timely inclusion are distinct. [Default rate](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/mining/src/mempool/config.rs#L17-L20), [relay calculation](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/mining/src/mempool/check_transaction_standard.rs#L67-L104).

Necessary arithmetic consequences for a 60M-sompi singleton:

- At 500, the direct verifier/key charge alone exceeds its entire value by **13.75M**. A 10M minimum payout requires at least **83.75M** available before additional costs.
- Even ignoring overhead, spending all 60M permits at most `60M / 147,500 ≈ 406.78` sompi/g; retaining a 10M payout permits at most **338.98**. These are necessary bounds, not sufficient admission/inclusion guarantees.
- A 1% fee cap on a hypothetical 10-KAS payment is 10M sompi. The direct verifier alone fits that cap only at rates at most **67.80**, below the inspected 100-sompi/g default. A larger note can cure balance insufficiency without curing that payment-fee target.

Earlier retained full-transaction results remain separate: direct v6 at budget 1,600 reached mass 191,628 in its worst measured mode; the old one-shot J336 diagnostic reached 163,290; the fresh continuing-profile pair reached 196,484, quoted 98.242M at 500 against its unchanged actual 80M fee. The pair's actual fee remains 40M per inner bundle; a cheaper necessary quote is not a reduction in paid authorized fees. A pair contains two bundles/four shielded outputs, not four independent payments. No complete fee target, accepted backing or owner exit follows from the local validation. [Closed checkpoint](../intensive-2026-10-22/FINAL-CHECKPOINT.md), [v6 adoption decision](../../docs/adr/0036-v6-native-budget-1600.md).

## What established protocols actually change

### Orchard: native action proof and native fee policy

Current checked pins: Zcash ZIPs `2bdf39494320e5e04057ac93b1794031ebfb00b5`; Orchard `616a669df8c9a59c33d47064d3ce04b25d026b2e`. ZIP-317 specifies conventional wallet/miner policy, not consensus: `5,000 * max(2, logical_actions)` zatoshi, with Orchard contributing its Action count. A pure two-Action Orchard transaction conventionally costs 10,000 zatoshi; adding a normal transparent withdrawal output to two padded Actions raises the action sum and conventional fee to 15,000. These are ZEC amounts/policy units, not Kaspa mass. [ZIP-317](https://github.com/zcash/zips/blob/2bdf39494320e5e04057ac93b1794031ebfb00b5/zips/zip-0317.rst#L110-L202).

One Halo2 proof covers the Action bundle; ZIP-224 explicitly distinguishes this from recursion. A holder can create a bundle without another spender's participation. Native Zcash validation understands the proof, nullifiers and `valueBalanceOrchard` turnstile; unshielding exposes value. Placing analogous predicates inside a Kaspa Groth16 circuit retains Kaspa's verifier charge. Equivalent native Halo2/state validation would be a different consensus architecture, not a current tagged precompile. [ZIP-224 proof construction](https://github.com/zcash/zips/blob/2bdf39494320e5e04057ac93b1794031ebfb00b5/zips/zip-0224.rst#L110-L125), [turnstile boundary](https://github.com/zcash/zips/blob/2bdf39494320e5e04057ac93b1794031ebfb00b5/zips/zip-0224.rst#L235-L251).

### Monero: noncooperating decoys and native confidential accounting

Current checked Monero head is `8340be5e920d881edc006e0896d6074cd06dfb2e`. A spender selects public decoy outputs without needing those owners to sign. Its wallet fee is quantized transaction-weight times a dynamic fee rate; weight includes a Bulletproof aggregation clawback, not merely raw proof bytes. Native nodes validate key images, ring authorization and confidential balances. These are native XMR rules, not a KAS reserve-release mechanism. [Fee formula](https://github.com/monero-project/monero/blob/8340be5e920d881edc006e0896d6074cd06dfb2e/src/wallet/wallet2.cpp#L310-L316), [weight](https://github.com/monero-project/monero/blob/8340be5e920d881edc006e0896d6074cd06dfb2e/src/cryptonote_basic/cryptonote_format_utils.cpp#L80-L97), [official ring explanation](https://www.getmonero.org/resources/moneropedia/ringsignatures.html).

Ordinary signatures cannot replace this predicate: CLSAG binds the same hidden ring member to the relevant key/commitment relation and key image. Monero separately checks commitment balance and Bulletproof+ ranges. Independent Schnorr signatures establish none of those hidden-value/ring relations by themselves. [CLSAG verifier](https://github.com/monero-project/monero/blob/8340be5e920d881edc006e0896d6074cd06dfb2e/src/ringct/rctSigs.cpp#L888-L996), [balance/range checks](https://github.com/monero-project/monero/blob/8340be5e920d881edc006e0896d6074cd06dfb2e/src/ringct/rctSigs.cpp#L1414-L1456).

### MWEB: one-sided use, consensus aggregation and a native peg

Checked Litecoin `v0.21.5.8` is `ec1b6489a900d09cf5991e220dce089c77a232a2`. Current MWEB supports one-sided transactions: do not repeat the obsolete receiver-interaction requirement from the earlier LIP-003 design. Users need not negotiate a joint batch; miners construct the consensus aggregate/HogEx. Peg-out amount and script must match the validated MWEB data, with six-block maturity. [LIP-004](https://github.com/litecoin-project/lips/blob/73cb2a6cea05b3891e7986ca215e7ccf4a49cb41/lip-0004.mediawiki#L13-L21), [current peg rules](https://github.com/litecoin-project/litecoin/blob/ec1b6489a900d09cf5991e220dce089c77a232a2/doc/mweb/consensus.md#L130-L151).

Its fee code charges transparent byte fees plus 100 litoshi per MWEB weight unit. A standard output has weight 18, a stealth kernel 3, and a peg-out adds `ceil(script_bytes / 42)`; a normal change-plus-peg-out estimate can therefore have weight 22 before the transparent output byte fee. This is native Litecoin policy, not a Kaspa conversion rate. MWEB shows that protocol-native aggregation can avoid *user batching cooperation*, but requires native consensus to enforce its commitments, range proofs and peg. Running MWEB-like logic inside an existing Kaspa guest leaves the same native proof check. [Fee code](https://github.com/litecoin-project/litecoin/blob/ec1b6489a900d09cf5991e220dce089c77a232a2/src/policy/feerate.cpp#L11-L87), [weight code](https://github.com/litecoin-project/litecoin/blob/ec1b6489a900d09cf5991e220dce089c77a232a2/src/libmw/include/mw/consensus/Weight.h), [wallet estimate](https://github.com/litecoin-project/litecoin/blob/ec1b6489a900d09cf5991e220dce089c77a232a2/src/mweb/mweb_transact.cpp#L60-L94).

### Aztec: previously settled withdrawal messages, fee payers and escape

Checked released Aztec `v5.2.0` resolves to `49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6`; current development `next` is `e63137d734ef3d0d4d7c031ded0370b5168df800`. Neither observation establishes deployment. Released fee code allocates checkpoint publication/blob and epoch verification expenses across mana; aggregation shares those expenses rather than erasing them. [Fee allocation](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6/l1-contracts/src/core/libraries/rollup/FeeLib.sol#L269-L304).

The important counterexample to “every terminal exit needs a SNARK” is its Outbox. Only the rollup can insert a root. A later claim verifies recipient/chain, accepted root, Merkle membership and an unspent message ID; the example token portal consumes that authenticated message and pays its bound recipient/amount. The final claim needs no fresh SNARK because authorization was proved earlier. The portal source is an example under `test/portals`, not a deployment assertion. [Outbox](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6/l1-contracts/src/core/messagebridge/Outbox.sol#L103-L169), [example portal](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6/l1-contracts/test/portals/TokenPortal.sol#L126-L149).

**Inference:** an already authenticated payout entitlement could support a cheaper native terminal claim. This requires Kaspa-specific reserve/liability conservation, nullification, state/payout binding and available witnesses; it is not established by our current experiment. For an exit that is not yet settled, the owner still needs an accepted settlement, bearing its singleton proof cost or relying on shared settlement. Moving the charge earlier is not a reduction of the entire independent path.

Fee Payment Contracts change who pays and potentially the payment asset/visibility; a sponsored fee still has a payer. The bonded periodic escape hatch replaces committee block production but must still propose and prove checkpoints. Its `WITHDRAWAL_TAX` concerns leaving the hatch candidate set, not every user's withdrawal. Neither is a cheap owner-only native reserve escape. [Official fee-payment guide](https://docs.aztec.network/developers/docs/aztec-js/how_to_pay_fees), [hatch overview](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6/l1-contracts/src/core/EscapeHatch.sol#L15-L30), [proof checks](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6/l1-contracts/src/core/EscapeHatch.sol#L280-L362), [candidate exit](https://github.com/AztecProtocol/aztec-packages/blob/49a592109ec4f18d79212b43d621891aaf36f7b6/l1-contracts/src/core/EscapeHatch.sol#L221-L242).

### RAILGUN: private gas compensation is still a cost

Checked contract `main` remains `36bcf5ed7cf94bfafb6e1a303e1832c769c16780`; engine `main` remains `6e2614d53a106dd62abad91e7ce03ee4a3956138`. Broadcasters pay public-chain gas and receive private compensation plus a service premium. Shield/unshield treasury fees are separately documented at 0.25%. Neither mechanism reduces the chain's verification tariff. [Broadcasters](https://docs.railgun.org/developer-guide/wallet/broadcasters), [fees](https://docs.railgun.org/community-faqs/readme/costs-and-fees).

The V2 transaction-array entrypoint iterates its entries and verifies each entry's proof before nullification/unshield transfers. Grouping transactions/tree updates is not recursive verification elimination. PPOI is separate from the core contract and its standby return path does not waive gas. [Array validation](https://github.com/Railgun-Privacy/contract/blob/36bcf5ed7cf94bfafb6e1a303e1832c769c16780/contracts/logic/RailgunSmartWallet.sol#L97-L188), [proof call](https://github.com/Railgun-Privacy/contract/blob/36bcf5ed7cf94bfafb6e1a303e1832c769c16780/contracts/logic/RailgunLogic.sol#L420-L475), [verifier](https://github.com/Railgun-Privacy/contract/blob/36bcf5ed7cf94bfafb6e1a303e1832c769c16780/contracts/logic/Verifier.sol#L87-L123), [PPOI distinction](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/assurance/private-proofs-of-innocence).

## Current Kaspa choices and the condition each changes

| Direction | What is real now | What it cannot claim under the original conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Smaller proof/seal, tighter budget, faster prover | Existing compression and measured budget reductions remove overhead | Cannot remove a fresh verifier's fixed 140k base. Our latest valid pair still pays actual 80M and fails its 500-rate quote. |
| Recursive/batch settlement | Existing RISC0/Groth16 route can authenticate multiple bundles | Average `shared settlement cost / B` assumes those B operations are actually available. No guarantee for B=1 or a lone 60M holder; four outputs are not four payments. |
| Pre-settled claim/output | Aztec demonstrates the authorization-then-Merkle-claim pattern | Earlier proof and liability/payout enforcement must already have happened. A public entitlement changes observability; this is not a current reviewed native-KAS exit implementation. |
| Larger minimum balance / restricted fee-demand domain | Explicit economic policy can require enough native KAS for fees+payout | Solves solvency only over the stated rate range; changes the 60M product condition and does not ensure 1% payments or arbitrary-congestion liveness. |
| Explicit self top-up / fee credit / sponsor | Moves payment to another declared balance/payer | Does not reduce work or fees. Violates an assumption of no extra funding if the singleton itself must cover everything. Credit must remain a conserved refundable liability, never hidden principal subsidy. |
| One-time native recipient keys | Standard KAS P2PK spends need only ordinary native signatures | Offers narrower address-reuse protection with public amounts/outpoint graph; not shielded sender/value privacy. BIP-352 is a Bitcoin-specific comparator, not a drop-in reviewed Kaspa protocol. |
| New/cheaper native verifier or native confidential-transaction rules | Orchard/Monero/MWEB show established system families | Requires new consensus/verifier/state support or tariff review, not an available application-only optimization. The asset may remain native KAS, but “current primitives” has changed. |

The KIP-17 interface supplies signatures, hashes, bounded arithmetic and covenant introspection; its listed ZK route delegates to KIP-16. Current native tags do not expose Halo2, CLSAG or Bulletproof verification, nor general elliptic-curve group operations as interchangeable application APIs. A new script-level verifier might be a research question, but this review has no established implementation, validated cost bound or security argument making it a usable bypass. Do not substitute individual signatures for hidden-membership/value predicates. [KIP-17](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/e4ae2332117b5cb68bd6188e065ef885b6d17939/kip-0017.md#L65-L91), [standard KAS P2PK](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/blob/4edcbbccb449f84832afb1ca157904b35f37d006/crypto/txscript/src/standard.rs#L19-L23), [Bitcoin-specific BIP-352](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/c0c5aea41e6a334d34db4ca87ae53bbfd7f268d1/bip-0352.mediawiki#L1-L61).

## vProgs and public issue evidence: useful limits

The current vProgs covenant preserves `output[0].value == input[0].value`. Its permission-exit output is explicitly funded by the settler's own signed inputs alongside the fee, rather than reducing covenant value. This is not evidence that a holder can independently withdraw native backing from a shielded reserve, and the funding cannot be omitted from economic comparisons. Its generic Groth16 wrapper still uses the native five-scalar route. [Value/permission checks](https://github.com/kaspanet/vprogs/blob/f9b84a863a7c7c20586a9cf947550475e894f72e/zk/backend/risc0/covenant/src/script.rs#L782-L789), [explicit settler-funded explanation](https://github.com/kaspanet/vprogs/blob/f9b84a863a7c7c20586a9cf947550475e894f72e/zk/backend/risc0/covenant/src/script.rs#L900-L911), [wrapper](https://github.com/kaspanet/vprogs/blob/f9b84a863a7c7c20586a9cf947550475e894f72e/zk/backend/risc0/covenant/src/script.rs#L950-L1019).

## Decision consequence

For a newly settled singleton that retains a fresh G16 authorization check, the 60M/500-rate condition is already falsified by source arithmetic. Another proof-generation benchmark cannot change that conclusion. Batching can be tested honestly as a separate cost/capacity option, with availability and actual authorized fees visible; it cannot close independent singleton exit liveness.

The most useful unresolved design question is whether the product can accept a separately authenticated withdrawal entitlement with a cheap final claim, and where its earlier proof cost, data availability and native reserve liability are paid/enforced. Otherwise a requirement must explicitly change: economic minimum/rate range, funding source, privacy objective, or native consensus support. This review selects none of those architectures and authorizes no implementation of them.

The frozen public-price prior floor 21.09772557% versus 20% cap also remains a workload information limit in the retained experiment, not a prover/runtime bug repaired by a cheaper verifier. Financial privacy and economical liveness remain separate unclosed gates. [Preserved checkpoint limits](../intensive-2026-10-22/FINAL-CHECKPOINT.md).

**Current-repo authority is the material gap.** The v6 note opening is `(owner, incoming, value, rho)`, with:

```text
owner = SHA256(OWNER_TAG || domain || wallet.spending || incoming)
CM    = SHA256(NOTE_TAG || domain || owner || incoming || u64LE(value) || rho)
NF    = HMAC-SHA256(wallet.spending,
                   positioned_NF_TAG || domain || CM || u32LE(position))
```

These are the existing relations, not a proposed replacement. The wallet's native payout seed is independent of `wallet.spending`; the note does not thereby commit an ordinary native Schnorr payout key. A sender can construct the note opening from public receive data, so possession of that opening alone is not spend authority. A signature under an independently supplied native key does not establish the existing secret-hash ownership or HMAC-nullifier relation. [Note/receive/wallet source](../../poc/shared-pool/src/protocol.rs), [actual v6 ownership and NF constraints](../../poc/shared-pool/src/two_input/circuit.rs), [positioned NF derivation](../../poc/shared-pool/src/compact/mod.rs).

Revealing `wallet.spending` would make those hash relations transparent, but discloses a reusable wallet secret rather than merely this note. Same-key note authority and historical/future nullifier privacy would be affected; preventing payout redirection remains essential even when the opening is public. No reviewed hash/signature-only branch establishes the required binding without that disclosure; the existing private branch establishes it using its proof. This is not a theorem against a differently specified note format with established public-key authority; changing the current format/relation/policy would need explicit review.

Any such branch must consume the **identical existing NF in the current authenticated set**, with ordinary/paired cross-branch exclusion, current reserve and exact owner-bound payout/fee conservation. Membership alone is not unspentness. A separate “emergency spent” marker is insufficient. Existing v6/S7 count parity, indexed-set ordering/capacity and immutable successor policy also remain obligations; current v6 consumes two NF slots even for a one-real-note exit. An old immutable covenant does not gain another branch from this note. [Current state/NF rules](../../poc/shared-pool/src/two_input/tree.rs), [paired/direct obligations](../../docs/rfc/0018-paired-native-settlement-experiment.md).

**Prior requirements permit disclosure to be considered, not silently accepted.** RFC-0012 explicitly allows an immediate potentially revealing emergency exit, while requiring its effect on ordinary historical privacy targets to remain in the observation horizon. ADR-0021's private saved fallback is still an existing proof, not a transparent-opening path; ADR-0022 rejected publishing an opening for its bounded confidentiality question. I-3/I-4/I-7 still require authority, single spend and withdrawal conservation. Revealing position/value—and especially a reusable key—could fail unchanged durable-privacy/private-witness gates. [RFC-0012](../../docs/rfc/0012-useful-privacy-requirements.md), [ADR-0021](../../docs/adr/0021-separate-exit-fallback-and-privacy-policy.md), [ADR-0022](../../docs/adr/0022-compact-two-note-deposits.md), [invariants](../../SECURITY-INVARIANTS.md).

No mass/fee bound is qualified for this unimplemented branch. Avoiding G16 removes that particular tariff assumption; it does not establish an affordable full transaction, current-state data availability, secure destination authorization or acceptable privacy. This addendum records a real omitted design question and its current-format obstacle. It selects no fallback, relaxes no gate, and performs no code, proof, owner, DRAIN or replay work.
