# Workload review — persistent balances and withdrawals


**Planning status:** experimental recommendations below are superseded by DECISION-CONTRACT.md; the cash-coupling proposal is not adopted and must not launch.

2026-10-22, Australia/Sydney. Read-only project review on Forge; no experiment, scoring, private evaluation data, system change or source edit. This report is a proposal for later work, not a frozen or authorized execution contract.

**Finding:** persistent shielded spending remains a reasonable research use case, but the reviewed evidence does not establish a default withdrawal policy. The largest unresolved workload dependency is whether native liquidity needs are independent of recent receipts. Keep the rejected partial-withdrawal policy rejected. Finish the current frozen 512-ledger test unchanged; its result cannot settle user behavior or the effect of activity density.

## What is supported, and what is chosen

The owner adopted repeated payments from an existing shielded balance, with recipients able to retain and spend receipts. That is a product requirement, not evidence that people will pre-fund a wallet or defer cash needs. Ordinary cashout, urgent exit and historical privacy through complete drainage remain explicit requirements [S1].

The existing relation permits private change plus a public native payout. Public payout arithmetic is therefore relevant regardless of a price prior. In the prior native usage history, six next-withdrawal exact guesses were supported by actual receipt withdrawals; other successful guesses matched unrelated receipts. The later partial-withdrawal screen improved one overlapping independent-demand history, failed its sparse gate, and made the declared receipt-correlated cases worse. These results support testing dependencies, not choosing the favorable cell [S3, S5].

External primary sources support the mechanisms, not the numerical workload. [ZIP-315](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0315), read 2026-10-22 and still Draft, recommends long-term shielded storage, describes transparent funding linkage, and warns against receipt-triggered on-chain behavior in its migration guidance. Those are design recommendations, not observed cash-demand frequencies. [Kappos et al., USENIX Security 2018](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-kappos.pdf), §§6.3–7, examines distinctive-value round trips and how internal transfers complicate their identification. Its January 2018 Zcash dataset does not estimate KPI users, present-day Kaspa usage, independent ownership or a suitable simulation distribution.

## Three sensitivity axes that can change the next decision

1. **Receipt-coupled liquidity and repeated cash needs — recommended next.** Compare the existing independent regime with a declared mixture in which eight of 32 request slots use the existing receipt/half-amount rule. Keep ordinary payment obligations, capital assignments and clock sequence matched across regimes. This is a benchmark stress level, not an estimate that 25% of users behave this way. It also introduces repeated requests for some owners. Report that the cash-demand distribution changes: this is regime sensitivity, not causal isolation of one scalar correlation. **Decision:** if modest declared coupling removes the partial policy's benefit or increases amount disclosure, do not pursue partial withdrawals as a general privacy policy. Retained balances can remain a conditional use-case hypothesis; users must still be free to exit immediately. A favorable result does not revive the old rejected gate.

2. **Price recurrence beyond a marginal price list.** On development material, define an iid-price arm and a repeated-counterparty price arm using the same exact per-history multiset of prices, with a predeclared deterministic assignment procedure. Preserve published support and all easy targets; give a prior only the catalogue/history information actually declared available. This is a synthetic sensitivity test, not invented retail data. **Decision:** if added predictability comes mainly from recurrent prices or permitted price history, redirect the claim/evidence work toward conditional priors and supported usage assumptions rather than infer a required payout redesign from common-price hits. If a transaction advantage survives both, a pure price-coincidence explanation weakens. No threshold or favorable distribution is substituted into old results.

3. **Clock sparsity with the economic history held fixed.** Replay identical roles, payments, amounts, cash requests, capitals, fees and final order under the two already-published gap schedules. Keep the one-minute cash gap explicit; no extra payments or participants appear. **Decision:** a difference supports a timing sensitivity of the fixed attack under these clocks; no difference weakens the interpretation that the prior overlap/sparse contrast was caused by timing. Either outcome leaves sparse honest ownership, low bankroll and changing membership open. It cannot justify a timer, compulsory delay or minimum-participant exit condition.

Funding provenance, low buffers and population composition should be separately varied later, not folded into these comparisons and called a timing or cash-correlation effect. The current directory-informed, fully pre-funded cohort must remain in every stated scope until that work exists.

## Minimal prospective plan for axis 1

This is a concrete workload proposal for the lead and statistical lane to turn into a separately frozen contract after the active run. Nothing below is a launch instruction.

- **Development/fresh split:** use only saved public old evidence and eight explicitly public-seed development master histories to implement shared random streams and validate the two request regimes. Existing frozen test results remain their own evaluation; do not reuse its seeds or histories. Then freeze sources, request transformation, observers, target eligibility, inference/uncertainty plan, costs and all hashes before any fresh seed. No selecting the mixture weight from accuracies.
- **Paired design:** each master history supplies the same 64 original payments, prices, starting funds and final owner order. Use q=0 and q=8/32 receipt-coupled request slots, selected uniformly without replacement from the declared slots before replay. Remaining slots retain the original independent request rows. Run whole and partial policies on identical obligations within each regime. Keep the two urgent exit times and owners fixed from the original independent plan across regimes, as separate obligations, so changing the request owner does not silently change the exit stress. Keep complete drainage and exact native cash accounting. Owners/sizes of ordinary cash requests differ between regimes by design; disclose this explicitly.
- **Budget:** exactly 128 fresh independent master histories, hence 512 ledger replays (two regimes × two policies). At final horizon, two views give 1,024 observer jobs; creation/next-payout diagnostics and their retained-prefix strategies must be fully specified before freezing. This budget is an operational proposal, not a claimed power calculation. Statistical review must set the exact multiplicity-aware independent-ledger inference before sampling. If it cannot resolve a margin, report unresolved; do not add histories after seeing results.
- **Controls:** equal accounting and obligations within each policy pair; all positive notes and overshoot retained; every urgent exit and final drain completed; request-regime construction exactly matches the manifest; no receipt/owner/truth label leaks to observers. Include public known-answer full-receipt and singleton/all-other-users-adversarial controls, integer/fee/floor and repeated-request controls, and a neutral retiming control with no economics changed. A control failure blocks evaluation; unmet obligations or invalid accounting are not privacy successes and may not be fixed by resampling.
- **Observers and predictions:** retain the existing attack family; no new attack implementation is proposed here. C+D and eight-pooled are primary; freeze separate named-participant diagnostics before launch. Give every view its legitimately known policy and records, never hidden target masks or honest requests. Seal every prediction before scoring. Compare selected fixed strategies and matched baselines without per-target oracle winners; let the statistical lane specify which contrasts are inferential. Keep exact, approximate, owner/relationship and balance/ancestry findings separate.
- **Decision/requirements retained:** preserve the original failed screen, 20% amount cap, five-point advantage cap, identity and selective-targeting caps, zero forced-disclosure requirement, integer sompi, fee/resource targets and independent exits. Preserve all safety invariants and all failed/incomplete cases. No mandatory retention, subsidy, invented cover, privileged rescue or new token. The result can reject a general policy interpretation or justify a later question; it cannot itself qualify native privacy, recovery or mainnet deployment.
